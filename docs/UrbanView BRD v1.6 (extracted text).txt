1. What We Are Building 
UrbanView tells you what you can build on a piece of land, and whether it is worth building. Today that answer takes days: 
planning rules sit in PDFs across different institutions, cadastral data sits elsewhere, market prices sit with agencies. 
Someone has to assemble it by hand, and mostly nobody does. 
The user opens a map of Podgorica and clicks a plot. The system identifies the parcel, works out which planning zone 
governs it, and shows what the adopted plan permits — land use, maximum floors, site coverage, floor area ratio. It then 
calculates the money: buildable floor area, construction cost, market value, profit, ROI. Under two seconds, no login. If 
they want more than the automated answer, they pay for an expert analysis of that site, delivered by email. 
This build is 2D and informative. 3D, AR, subscriptions and scenario comparison are excluded and belong to later phases. 
What it has to prove: that developers and architects will use a map-based feasibility tool, and that some will pay for a 
deeper look. 
The flow in plain language 
•​ User opens urbanview.io. Map of Podgorica loads with planning zone boundaries visible. No account. 
•​ User finds a site — address, map click, or parcel number. 
•​ System identifies the cadastral parcel, the planned urban parcel, and the governing zone. 
•​ Panel opens: identification, then planning parameters, then market data and ROI. All financials as ranges. 
•​ User toggles layers, or keeps browsing for a better plot. 
•​ User optionally orders the expert report: short form, one-off payment, emailed manually by an expert. 
 
Two entry states, both must work. Some users arrive with a site and want to know what it permits. Others have no site 
and are hunting the city for one worth buying. The first needs search; the second needs browsing. 
2. Features to Build 
Scope and cooperation model. The functional prototype budget is EUR 10,000; the next phase will be funded at a 
six-figure level. Because the feature set below has grown during review beyond what EUR 10,000 covers, the client 
leaves Artech Digital to choose the cooperation model: either pay-per-project as a subcontractor, with the delivered 
scope aligned to the budget, or an associated-partner arrangement, where Artech is paid partly in fees and partly in 
equity in exchange for delivering the larger scope. Which features land in the prototype versus the next phase follows 
from that choice. 
2.1 Map and Layer Engine 
2D interactive map of Podgorica with switchable vector layers. Planning zone boundaries are the core layer and the 
default view — the first thing a user should see is how the city divides into zoning plans, because each zone carries 
different constraints. All non-core layers toggle independently, and are selected by clicking their visual representation — a 
layer card or icon — rather than checkboxes, radio buttons or eye icons. Map features are selectable and data-bearing, 
not flat imagery. The mapping provider (Mapbox or OpenStreetMap) is to be discussed and chosen on cost and capability.

2.2 Parcel Identification and the Existing-vs-Planned Problem 
The most important technical requirement in this document. The parcel in the cadastre today and the parcel envisaged by 
the adopted plan are frequently not the same — land gets taken for roads, public space, reparcelling. The client's 
example: a plot recorded at 100 units of area is reduced to 75 by the plan. The developer builds on 75. In most cases the 
planned urban parcel differs from the original cadastral parcel in both geometry and surface area — and this is not 
common knowledge; typically only the profession is aware of it. Surfacing it clearly is a core value of the platform, not a 
detail.

Market data source (confirmed): publicly available real estate listings (Realitica, Estitor) combined with Monstat official 
statistics for selling prices and construction cost indicators. The cadastral institution is not the market source — and the

include market values, but they carry lower weight than current real estate listings and Monstat statistics. Portals plus 
Monstat are the primary reference for land values, apartment sale prices and construction costs. 
User-editable assumptions (confirmed): users can adjust assumptions such as construction cost, selling price and 
saleable-area percentage to test their own scenarios. The client considers this a valuable option. Edited values recalculate 
the ranges live; the deterministic formulas are unchanged. 
The engine is deterministic. AI does not generate the financial arithmetic — the client's words: "mathematically correct, 
not just AI assumptions." AI may interpret and normalise reference prices from listings and Monstat into the input 
values, but the calculation itself stays formula-driven.

The prototype has a second objective beyond validating the user workflow: establishing a repeatable data-preparation 
methodology that can be replicated across Montenegro and other countries. The full Montenegro application would 
cover 5–8 municipalities including Podgorica — more than 80% of the country's construction activity. Build the ingestion 
pipeline as a repeatable process, not a one-off Podgorica script. 
2.6 AI Document Extraction and Expert Approval 
AI has one job: read the source documents so humans do not transcribe them. It handles automatic extraction of planning 
parameters from planning documents and AI-assisted interpretation of those documents, structures the parameters 
against the zone and block schema, and attaches a source reference to every value. It also interprets reference prices from 
real estate listings and Monstat data, using them as reference values for land prices, apartment sale prices and 
construction costs. Input data and some calculations may be prepared manually where that is faster. AI feeds reference 
values into the engine but does not perform the financial arithmetic, and does not publish anything unreviewed. 
Expert review: 100% of AI-extracted planning information is reviewed and approved before publication. A prototype-stage 
control only — the client accepts it will not scale beyond one city and will be replaced by a sampling regime. Textual 
accuracy matters as much as numerical: a misread planning term changes the meaning of a constraint. 
Most planning source data is PDF — documents authored for print, not machines. Converting them into usable 
georeferenced geometry is the largest technical risk and cost in this build. The client noted on the call that a surveying or 
GIS engineer will probably be needed. Treat that as a probable additional cost, not an edge case.

What the paid report adds (client-confirmed): the expert-led report provides site analysis, expert interpretation of the

2.8 AI Assistant (Freemium) 
A chat-like AI assistant that helps users explore the available information. Where the paid expert report is 
human-produced and deep, the assistant is automated and conversational: it answers questions about a selected location, 
explains what a planning parameter means, and points the user to the source. It is freemium — three AI-assisted 
interactions per session are free as part of the acquisition hook; unlimited interactions are a paid subscription (one of the 
three revenue channels in 2.7). 
•​ Scope: reads the source documents and input data and answers questions grounded in them — so it is limited to, 
and effectively trained on, UrbanView's own approved data: planning parameters, zone information, and the figures 
shown in the panel. Additional context documents may be provided to broaden it — laws, regulations, building 
codes, news — since the assistant needs slightly wider context than the parcel data alone. It does not invent planning 
values or generate the financial arithmetic. 
•​ Guardrails: the same controls as the rest of the platform — it draws only on expert-approved data and cites the 
source document, and it does not present unreviewed extractions as fact. 
•​ Boundary: the assistant helps users navigate and understand; it does not perform the expert interpretation, risk 
assessment or development-scenario work reserved for the paid report. 
New scope. The AI assistant is a feature the client added during review. It runs on a freemium model — three free 
interactions per session, unlimited on subscription. If built on a third-party service (Gemini, ChatGPT, DeepSeek, etc.) the 
free tier is cheap to run; a full custom AI solution is the paid subscription channel. Either way it introduces a 
conversational LLM surface over the platform's data and should be reflected in the estimate and timeline alongside the 
expanded layer set in Section 2.1. 
2.9 Admin Panel 
Role-gated, separate from the public platform. This is where the data pipeline is operated. 
•​ Upload and version planning documents, GIS files and cadastral extracts 
•​ Review, amend and approve AI-extracted data before it publishes 
•​ Create and edit zone parameter sets; manage which areas are live 
•​ Update financial assumptions — construction rates, market benchmarks, range boundaries 
•​ View and assign paid analysis orders 
Audit logging of changes and approvals is unresolved. Recommendation: build it. Once a published value is wrong, the 
first question is who changed what and when. 
2.10 Behavioural Data Capture 
Selective capture of location queries, parcel selections and layer interactions, for later resale as location-demand insight. 
The client will specify which interactions matter. Personal data limited to what a purchaser submits. Privacy notice 
required at launch; legal basis for commercial reuse still to be confirmed.

6. Additional Requirements 
6.1 Legal and Compliance 
•​ Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, Refund Policy and an indicative-figures disclaimer required at launch. Drafted by a 
lawyer familiar with Montenegrin consumer and data protection law 
•​ Data licensing: the platform commercially displays data derived from public planning documents and the cadastre. 
Permission per source is outstanding — blocks commercial launch, not the build 
•​ Liability: the platform publishes ROI figures people may act on. Ranges and visible assumptions mitigate this; a clear 
"indicative, not investment or planning advice" statement is required on every screen showing financials 
•​ A paid, human-produced analysis carries professional exposure of a different order to an automated estimate. The 
expert's standing and any indemnity cover should be established before the first order 
6.2 Analytics 
Tracked from day one — the prototype is a validation instrument, and untracked usage proves nothing. 
•​ map_loaded, search_performed, parcel_selected, layer_toggled 
•​ panel_viewed, financials_viewed, source_reference_opened 
•​ order_started, checkout_completed, return_visit, sessions_per_user 
Client dashboard: conversion at each funnel step, orders and revenue, most-searched districts, repeat usage rate. 
6.3 Support and Content 
•​ Email support to a shared inbox, 24 business hours. Short FAQ covering what the data means, where it comes from, 
what the paid analysis includes 
•​ Planning terminology glossary — the platform uses terms (IZ, FAR/II, cadastral municipality, urban block) that its own 
users define inconsistently. Plain-language definitions are a trust feature 
•​ Refund policy not yet defined. Recommendation: full refund before delivery, discretionary after. Needed before the 
first paying customer 
•​ 3–5 example outputs for the landing page as the primary trust signal

trained on planning documentation; 3D module with constraint-aware push-and-pull modelling; 3D city models and AR;

MMP: Urban Modeler with prompt-based modelling and GIS import; full cadastral integration and City Information

Architectural implication for this build: isolate market-specific ingestion and planning terminology from the core engine.

future-proofing worth paying for now. 
9. Methodology Pipeline (User’s step-by-step when analyzing the 
location/parcel) 
In the following text, we will explain our steps that we use everytime we analyze a parcel for purposes of offering our 
services or when we have contract to design the object. The methodology consists of: 
1. Step: Searching for the specific cadastral parcel in the urban plans that also contain existing condition and planned 
condition. First check is if the cadastral parcel is the same geometry and area as planned one - urban parcel. During 
that research we check the ownership status of the parcel. 
2. Step: We look for parts from urban planning PDF’s that contain specific information: 

===== PAGE 11 =====
a. Planned urban parcels - regulation and its relation to public areas 
b. Planned urban regulation - Area envisaged for site coverage by the object, building height and number of floors, 
linear distance from the neighboring parcels and public areas. 
c. Planned land use within the block or area that has the same markation 
d. Existing and planned infrastructure utilities - water, sewage, energy infrastructure, heating, access roads etc. 
3. Step: After analyzing the parcel and the referent PDF planning document, we start designing the orthogonal 
projection (2D view from the top) of the object to the parcel, implementing all the rules and regulations. 
4. Step: After the design of the object we start working on 3D view and we start fitting the object into the build 
environment. 
5. Step: This step is the final. After we conclude that it is the design that should be presented to the Client, we prepare 
preliminary 2D plans, 3D model and comprehensive analysis of real estate development venture. 
6. Step: If the Client decides to go for the venture, we prepare offer and start designing.
